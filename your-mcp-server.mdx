---
title: "Your own MCP server"
description: "How your own MCP server enforces your plans once you publish, and how you test it before then."
---

When your plugin runs its own MCP server, the `plugpass-implement-code-changes` skill adds an access layer to its code. The layer asks users to sign in and enforces your plans. It serves two paths.

## The canonical path

Your server's registered URL, usually ending in `/mcp`, is what real users connect to. It enforces access only once your plugin is **published**:

- **Before your first publish**, it works as your server did before Plugpass.
- **After you publish**, it asks users to sign in and enforces your plans, within five minutes of publishing.

## The test path

The same server at its URL plus `/test`, like `https://mcp.example.com/mcp/test`, asks users to sign in from your first deploy, and only your [test users](/test-users) can sign in to it. It runs the same tools and access checks as the canonical path, against your unpublished changes. The `plugpass-test-plugin` skill sets up a test version of your plugin that connects there, as [Testing](/test-users#testing) describes.

## Deploy, then test, then publish

Deploy your server before you publish. Deploying changes nothing for your users until you publish, so deploy whenever you like and test on the test path in the meantime. The publish page lays out the rest of each release in order, including when to push your plugin's changes.

## What a tool call uses

A paid tool's call uses up its allowance only when the tool succeeds. When it throws or returns an error, the user gets back what the call took.

A tool can also be limited by the things it handles instead of its uses, like the leads it returns or the leads it's sent. Sync finds these counts in your server's code, and your Plans page offers them as limits and credit costs. When a user has fewer left than a call finds, the result is trimmed to what they have left, with a note saying what was held back and how to get the rest.
