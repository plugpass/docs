---
title: "Plan data"
description: "Everything your pricing page needs to show your plans."
---

Your plan data describes your published plans: each plan's prices, credits, and features, and where its call-to-action button takes a visitor. Use it to build a pricing page on your own site that always matches what you've published.

## Fetch your plan data

With the Plugpass snippet on your pricing page, fetch your plan data on every page load:

```js
const planData = await window.plugpass.plans();
```

If your pricing page renders on the server or at build time, fetch the plan data from `/plans.json` on your Plugpass-hosted domain, like `https://get.coolplugin.com/plans.json`, then replace it with the response from `plugpass.plans()` so your page always reflects your published plans.

## Fields

<ResponseField name="preview" type="boolean">
  `true` when your pricing page was opened with **Preview pricing page** in your dashboard. The plan data then shows your unpublished changes.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="currency" type="string">
  The currency of every price. Always `usd`.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="billing_intervals" type="string[]">
  The billing intervals your plans offer, `monthly` first. When both are offered, let visitors choose between them.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="plans" type="object[]">
  Your plans, in order from least to most expensive.

  <Expandable title="properties" defaultOpen>
    <ResponseField name="slug" type="string">
      The plan's name as it appears in URLs, like `pro`. It changes when you rename the plan. For a plan with `credit_tiers`, it's the cheapest tier's slug.
    </ResponseField>

    <ResponseField name="name" type="string">
      The plan's name.
    </ResponseField>

    <ResponseField name="description" type="string | null">
      The plan's description, or `null` if it has none.
    </ResponseField>

    <ResponseField name="is_free" type="boolean">
      `true` for your free plan.
    </ResponseField>

    <ResponseField name="prices" type="object[]">
      One price for each billing interval. A free plan has a price of `0` on every billing interval, so you can show every plan the same way. For a plan with `credit_tiers`, these are the cheapest tier's prices.

      <Expandable title="properties" defaultOpen>
        <ResponseField name="interval" type="string">
          `monthly` or `annual`.
        </ResponseField>

        <ResponseField name="amount_cents" type="number">
          The price of the whole interval, in cents. An annual price is the price of a year, so divide it by 12 to show a monthly price.
        </ResponseField>

        <ResponseField name="plan_selected_url" type="string | null">
          Where the plan's call-to-action button takes the visitor for this interval, or `null` when it's their current plan and interval.
        </ResponseField>

        <ResponseField name="change_type" type="string">
          What choosing this plan and interval means for the visitor: `signup`, `upgrade`, `downgrade`, or `current_plan`. See [Change types](#change-types).
        </ResponseField>
      </Expandable>
    </ResponseField>

    <ResponseField name="credits" type="object | null">
      The credits the plan includes, or `null` if it includes none. For a plan with `credit_tiers`, the cheapest tier's credits.

      <Expandable title="properties" defaultOpen>
        <ResponseField name="limit_type" type="string">
          `monthly`, `total`, or `unlimited`. See [Limit types](#limit-types).
        </ResponseField>

        <ResponseField name="quantity" type="number | null">
          How many credits, or `null` when unlimited.
        </ResponseField>
      </Expandable>
    </ResponseField>

    <ResponseField name="credit_tiers" type="object[] | null">
      {/* TODO(copy): drafted with credit tiers. */}
      The credit amounts the plan is sold at, from least to most expensive, or `null` when the plan comes at one amount. Each tier is its own subscription with its own prices, so give the visitor a way to pick one, like a dropdown, and send them to the picked tier's `plan_selected_url`. A page that ignores tiers still works: the plan's own `slug`, `prices`, and `credits` are the cheapest tier's.

      <Expandable title="properties" defaultOpen>
        <ResponseField name="slug" type="string">
          The tier's name as it appears in URLs, like `pro-2000`. It changes when you rename the plan or change the tier's credits.
        </ResponseField>

        <ResponseField name="credits" type="object | null">
          The credits the tier includes, with the same `limit_type` and `quantity` as a plan's `credits`.
        </ResponseField>

        <ResponseField name="prices" type="object[]">
          One price for each billing interval, with the same fields as a plan's `prices`, including where the tier's call-to-action button takes the visitor and what choosing it means for them.
        </ResponseField>
      </Expandable>
    </ResponseField>

    <ResponseField name="features" type="object[]">
      Every feature the plan includes that you show on your pricing page, not only the ones it adds to the plan below it. An unrestricted feature, which is on none of your plans, appears on every plan as `unlimited`. A feature that uses credits appears on every plan with `credits`, as `credits`, and on no plan without them. Skills come first, then tools, then database records, each in alphabetical order.

      <Expandable title="properties" defaultOpen>
        <ResponseField name="id" type="string">
          The feature's ID. It stays the same when you rename the feature.
        </ResponseField>

        <ResponseField name="type" type="string">
          `skill`, `tool`, or `database_record`. A database record is something your users keep, like saved topics or documents, and its limit is how many they can have at once.
        </ResponseField>

        <ResponseField name="display_name" type="string">
          The feature's name as a label, like `Quick brainstorm`.
        </ResponseField>

        <ResponseField name="mid_sentence_name" type="string">
          The feature's name as it reads in the middle of a sentence, like `quick brainstorm`.
        </ResponseField>

        <ResponseField name="limit_type" type="string">
          `monthly`, `total`, `unlimited`, or `credits`. See [Limit types](#limit-types).
        </ResponseField>

        <ResponseField name="quantity" type="number | null">
          The number the limit allows, or `null` when unlimited or when the feature uses credits.
        </ResponseField>

        <ResponseField name="credit_cost" type="number | null">
          How many of the plan's credits one use costs, when the feature uses credits. `null` for a feature with a limit of its own. Show it as the feature's cost, like "5 credits per deep brainstorm use".
        </ResponseField>

        <ResponseField name="pitch" type="string | null">
          The end of a sentence describing the feature, or `null` if it has none. See [Feature pitches](#feature-pitches).
        </ResponseField>
      </Expandable>
    </ResponseField>
  </Expandable>
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="credit_costs" type="object[]">
  The features you show on your pricing page that use credits instead of a limit of their own, and what each use costs, in one list for the whole plugin. The same features are also in the `features` of every plan with `credits`, as `credits` with their `credit_cost`, so you can list them with the plan's other features.

  <Expandable title="properties" defaultOpen>
    <ResponseField name="id" type="string">
      The feature's ID. It stays the same when you rename the feature.
    </ResponseField>

    <ResponseField name="type" type="string">
      `skill` or `tool`.
    </ResponseField>

    <ResponseField name="display_name" type="string">
      The feature's name as a label.
    </ResponseField>

    <ResponseField name="credit_cost" type="number">
      How many credits one use costs.
    </ResponseField>
  </Expandable>
</ResponseField>

## Change types

Each price's `change_type` says what choosing that plan and interval means for the visitor, and its `plan_selected_url` takes them there.

| Value | When | Links to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `signup` | The free plan, for a visitor who isn't on a paid plan | Sign up |
| `upgrade` | A paid plan for a visitor who isn't on one, a higher plan or credit tier than the visitor's current one, or their current plan billed annually when they pay monthly | Checkout |
| `downgrade` | A lower plan or credit tier than the visitor's current one, including the free plan, or their current plan billed monthly when they pay annually | Billing settings |
| `current_plan` | The paid plan and interval the visitor is on | Nothing (`null`) |

Set each plan's call-to-action button to its `plan_selected_url` for the selected billing interval, and disable or hide the button for a `current_plan`. You can do the same for a `downgrade`.

<Note>
  Your pricing page only knows a visitor's current plan when they're signed in and your site shares a domain with your Plugpass-hosted pages, like `coolplugin.com` and `get.coolplugin.com`. Otherwise, every price is a `signup` or an `upgrade`, and checkout sends a visitor who is already on a paid plan to their billing settings.
</Note>

## Limit types

| Value | Quantity |
| --- | --- |
| `monthly` | How many uses, or credits, each month |
| `total` | How many uses, or credits, in total. For a database record, how many a user can have at once. |
| `unlimited` | `null` |
| `credits` | `null`. The feature uses the plan's credits; `credit_cost` says how many one use costs. |

## Feature pitches

A feature's `pitch` completes a sentence that starts with the feature's name:

| Type | Sentence | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `skill` or `tool` | `The {mid_sentence_name} {type} {pitch}.` | The quick brainstorm skill generates a short list of fresh ideas on any topic. |
| `database_record` | `{display_name} {pitch}.` | Saved topics keep the topics you want to come back to. |
